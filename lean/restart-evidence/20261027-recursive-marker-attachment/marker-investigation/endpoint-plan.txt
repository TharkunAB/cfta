Sibling restoration limit
NativeContinuations.run calls the completed head hook with its head cache. On hook completion, it runs the sibling suffix from the fixed captured before cache, while threading the hook's returned Caller and queries. See NativeContinuations.lean:40-70. Therefore a fresh marker created only in head A is not a marker of the captured input used by head B. Its raw ID can also be reused after restoration. An attachment theorem must quantify over outputs descended from the SAME hook invocation, not all later outputs of the ordinary scan or its final aggregate cache.

A structural witness recipe is two completed sibling heads whose hooks emit separately: A's output uses A's fresh marker; B starts at captured before and emits B's cache. The runtime equations do not preserve A's fresh slot into B. This is a source-level obstruction to a broad persistence argument. No full admitted native counterexample, Haskell defect or whole-output unsoundness claim is made here.
