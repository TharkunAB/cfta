Operational, numeric and semantic gates stay separate

Operational completion does not require Canonical, full assignment satisfaction or semantic retention/fresh-arity gates. It requires actual arity/project support and the explicit numeric Ranges. SelectedEdge.Ranges:84–91 asks traversal numeric admission at the actual successful own prefix. Traversal.Ranges:19–29 follows actual successful child endpoints. NativeBranchCompletion.Ranges:74–83 follows actual continuable sibling endpoints while retaining the original captured cache. These are conditional numeric capabilities, not mere finite-list lengths or unconditional initial-only capacity.

An existing stronger resource entry is CapturedEdge.complete_with_history_cache_bounds:31–90. At Caller(bits+2), it replaces Ranges by original Fits, Reserved at SelectedEdge.cost and guard Heap, keeps the entire old history output, and appends reached Fits/Heap and counter/mass bounds. The SelectedEdge resource wrapper has the same replacement. Reuse those as separate wrappers if original resource admission is desired; do not weaken them to future-room assumptions or claim a host-width guarantee. No resource premise may substitute for runtime branch correspondence.

Canonical preservation is a conditional actual-complete observer, not an output premise. CapturedEdge.complete_canonical:32–82 derives inherited ValidSuspended from Canonical plus the actual stored bucket; SelectedEdge.complete_canonical:32–91 derives projected trie validity from full Context's current annotation heap and passes valid descended buckets through actual traversal. It does not derive completion or numeric Ranges. NativeBranchCanonical adds Canonical before explicitly and concludes Canonical only for actual complete receipts. Query equality alone never reconstructs full Memos or Context.

Full captured assignment soundness needs additional real semantic gates: Canonical before, inherited no-terminal, fresh own arity and child bucket retention (CapturedEdgeSoundness:34–86). Full Context already provides the current normalized annotation heap; no new equivalent desired semantic premise is needed for that part. Mu/Union bucket omission is why retention remains substantive. Soundness is backward from an assignment satisfying an actual completed cache; it does not prove existence of such an assignment, completeness, rendering completion or whole-enumerator language. Diagonal families need genuine old admission plus common-bound alignment; diagonal observation alone is insufficient.

Ordered alternatives and later recursion

NativeBranches.run:30–43 restores the SAME captured cache/scs for every sibling, while passing reached native Caller/Queries. It continues after completed edges, arity rejection and valid raw[] rejection with some cache. Missing cache or unsupported/noncontinuable receipt stops before the suffix. Each processed receipt keeps its own endpoint/cache; do not retarget earlier receipts to the final native heap.

NativeBranchCompletion.complete_with_history:154–192 is the next whole-scan producer to retain once. It keeps full aggregate Post/Memos plus each processed receipt's own Post/inventory/full Memos/query, Admitted, conditional Pending and FragmentHistory. Its unsupported projection arm stops at some.unhandled; a separate support condition makes the whole scan complete. Old proof synchronization uses sums for r/n/k and maxima for p/h, and transports original captured-cache native realization/flags through headPost.effects.keeps. Preserve that exact caller threading rather than rerunning independent siblings at the original native heap.

This plain scan performs no fragment callback. For the actual recursive worker, NativeContinuations runs callback/recursive continuation before the next sibling, threads its native endpoint, and restores the same captured cache; sibling metadata stays branch-local. A flat legacy scan relation or identity-hook equality does not close that interleaving boundary. Full callback transport exists separately but ordinary/captured/hook/recursive composition must still be proved.
